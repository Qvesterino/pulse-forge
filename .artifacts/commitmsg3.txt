feat(fx): paint the core EQ response curve in the kit multiview

The EQ column showed four sliders for a device with 27 parameters, which is
the one panel a beatmaker reaches for first and the one place four sliders say
the least. It now paints its curve above them.

eqCurve is an offline transcription of the worklet's own arithmetic, NOT
BiquadFilterNode. eq-processor.js deliberately does not use textbook RBJ
shelves: they are PARALLEL form over 2-pole Butterworth prototypes so both
ends land exactly on 0 dB and +/-G, and peaking bands carry a bilinear Q
pre-correction. A BiquadFilter response would therefore draw a curve that is
close to, but not, the one the track is processing - for a level display that
is worse than no curve at all. The two must change together.

The spec pins the physics rather than smoothness: a flat passband, 3 dB at
each HP/LP corner (factory hpFreq is 20 Hz, so 0 dB at 20 Hz would be the
lie), and a +12 dB shelf that does NOT droop to +7 dB at 20 kHz - the exact
failure the parallel topology exists to prevent. It also records that the
analytic response floors at -60 dB while the display floors at -24, because
at Nyquist a 2-pole lowpass is around -40 dB and flooring it early collapses
two different responses onto one number.

The gate reads the curve's pixels, not its presence: dragging LOW SHELF must
change both the stored gain (0 dB to +13.5 dB) and the painted weight, and
one Ctrl+Z must restore both. It also gates REACHABILITY - the EQ column is
taller than its four siblings since it gained a curve, and a knob painted
below the fold is identical to a usable one until the user reaches for it.
Sizing it to fit (46px curve, 232px strip) was the fix; the first version of
that check compared an absolute Y to the viewport height, which says nothing
because the devices panel is nowhere near the bottom of the screen.
